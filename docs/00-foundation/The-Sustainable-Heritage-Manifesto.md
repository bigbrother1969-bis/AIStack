# The Sustainable Heritage Manifesto

*Draft v0.1*

> Engineering should not merely produce software.
> It should build a sustainable heritage of knowledge.

---

## Provenance

This artifact restores the manifesto to the Governed Heritage.

Until 2026-08-14 the file at this path contained no article of the manifesto. It
held a 53 KB conversation transcript in which the document was being reviewed,
including a proposed layout with three empty placeholders. The manifesto itself —
eighteen articles — existed only as a Word export outside the repository, dated
2026-07-11.

The source is French. Per FDN-0001, every Knowledge Artifact is written in English;
this is a translation, and the French export remains the author's original. Where the
manifesto and the existing heritage name the same idea, the heritage's established
terminology is used — *Governed Heritage*, *Single Point Of Truth*, *Knowledge
Artifact*, *Sustainable Heritage Engineering*.

The manifesto is conceptually upstream of FDN-0003. Its identifier is not numbered in
sequence, as its adoption predates the Foundation series.

---

## Preamble

For decades, software engineering has measured its success in features delivered,
versions released and systems deployed.

Yet software is only the visible part of a far wider intellectual effort.

Every architecture, every decision, every model, every operating procedure, every
diagnosis and every lesson learned constitutes knowledge.

And most of that knowledge disappears.

It is lost when engineers leave an organisation, when documentation goes stale, when
projects are rewritten, when technologies evolve, or when artificial intelligence
generates code without preserving the reasoning that produced it.

Technical debt is only a symptom. The real debt is the gradual loss of understanding.

We therefore propose a different objective: not to build better software, but to
build better knowledge.

Software becomes one of the expressions of a governed heritage of knowledge, whose
purpose is to outlive technologies, organisations, infrastructures and generations of
engineers.

This is the reason for being of Sustainable Heritage Engineering.

---

## Article I — Knowledge Is the Primary Asset

Knowledge is the true product of engineering.

Software, documentation, infrastructure, tests, diagrams and configuration are only
different representations of that knowledge.

Engineering succeeds when understanding progresses.

Implementation comes afterwards.

---

## Article II — Understanding Precedes Implementation

Implementation shall never be the starting point.

Understanding produces architecture.

Architecture organises knowledge.

Documentation preserves architecture.

Implementation becomes the natural consequence of that work.

Code is never the objective.

Code is the proof that understanding has become executable.

---

## Article III — Architecture Organises Complexity

Complexity cannot be removed.

It can only be organised.

Architecture exists to distribute complexity across coherent domains, with explicit
responsibilities and governed contracts.

Every architectural boundary protects understanding.

---

## Article IV — Knowledge Shall Be Governed

Knowledge without governance becomes an opinion.

Every governed knowledge element shall have:

- a Single Point Of Truth (SPOT);
- an owner;
- provenance;
- traceability;
- a quality level;
- a confidence level;
- a lifecycle;
- a version history.

Knowledge governance is an integral part of engineering.

---

## Article V — Knowledge Shall Outlive Technologies

Technologies evolve.

Languages disappear.

Frameworks become obsolete.

Artificial intelligence models are replaced.

Knowledge shall remain portable across all of these changes.

No essential knowledge shall depend on a particular vendor, cloud, framework or AI
model.

---

## Article VI — To Document Is to Engineer

Documentation is not produced after engineering.

Documentation is engineering.

Every significant architectural decision deserves explicit documentation.

Documentation preserves reasoning.

Reasoning preserves understanding.

Understanding preserves the heritage.

---

## Article VII — Artificial Intelligence Is a Reasoning Tool

Artificial intelligence does not replace engineering.

It accelerates reasoning.

It eases exploration.

It assists implementation.

It explains.

It documents.

It never becomes the authority.

Truth remains governed.

---

## Article VIII — Data Demands Governance

There is no artificial intelligence without data.

There is no reliable data without governance.

Every data element shall have:

- an owner;
- provenance;
- a quality level;
- traceability;
- governance.

Artificial intelligence amplifies the quality of data.

It cannot compensate for its absence.

---

## Article IX — Generated Artifacts Are Disposable

Generated artifacts are products.

Generators are the real assets.

When a generated artifact requires a correction, the engineering effort shall first
address its generator.

Maintenance belongs to the source of knowledge, not to its manifestations.

---

## Article X — Sustainable Engineering Reduces Cognitive Load

Human beings have limited memory.

Artificial intelligences have limited context.

Organisations have limited continuity.

Engineering shall therefore externalise understanding into governed knowledge rather
than entrust it to individual memory.

Knowledge shall be reconstructible.

---

## Article XI — Engineering Is an Act of Transmission

An engineer does not only solve today's problems.

They allow tomorrow's engineers to understand yesterday's solutions.

Transmission is a fundamental responsibility of engineering.

Knowledge that cannot be transmitted is incomplete knowledge.

---

## Article XII — Every Recommendation Shall Be Explainable

No recommendation shall appear as an unexplained conclusion.

Every recommendation shall identify:

- the observations;
- the evidence;
- the rules applied;
- the reasoning;
- the confidence level;
- the assumptions.

Explainability is a requirement.

---

## Article XIII — Heritage Before Performance

Performance matters.

Maintainability matters more.

Knowledge outlives optimisations.

No premature optimisation shall compromise architectural clarity.

The simplest architecture capable of preserving understanding shall always be
preferred.

---

## Article XIV — Sustainability Begins Before Implementation

Industrial quality is achieved mostly before implementation.

Engineering effort shall be invested upstream:

- in understanding;
- in conceptual modelling;
- in governance;
- in architecture;
- in documentation;
- in explicit contracts.

Implementation should read as already-matured code rather than as a first attempt.

---

## Article XV — Engineering Is a Permanent Discovery

Engineering begins with observation.

Observation produces understanding.

Understanding produces models.

Models produce architecture.

Architecture produces implementation.

Implementation produces new observations.

Knowledge progresses continuously through this cycle.

---

## Article XVI — The Heritage Shall Be Able to Rebuild Itself

A sustainable heritage of knowledge shall be able to rebuild itself.

Its documentation, its governance, its architecture and its operational understanding
shall make it possible to reconstruct the system in a new environment.

The ultimate objective is Self-Onboarding.

---

## Article XVII — Open Knowledge Is Sustainable Knowledge

Knowledge survives through openness.

- Open standards.
- Open documentation.
- Open governance.
- Open formats.
- Open ecosystems.

Independence from vendors is a property of sustainable engineering.

---

## Article XVIII — Engineering Serves the Common Good

Engineering shall favour:

- autonomy;
- transparency;
- sustainability;
- explainability;
- transmission;
- interoperability;
- long-term preservation.

Technology serves society.

Knowledge serves humanity.

---

## Final Declaration

Software becomes obsolete.

Technologies evolve.

Artificial intelligences change.

Organisations transform.

People leave.

Knowledge is the only engineering asset capable of surviving all of these changes.

Our responsibility is therefore not only to build systems.

It is to build a governed heritage of understanding that future generations will be
able to understand, enrich, evolve and pass on in their turn.

This is the reason for being of Sustainable Heritage Engineering.

This is the commitment of Sustainable Heritage Engineering.
