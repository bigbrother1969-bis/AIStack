# AIStack user manual

AIStack observes the infrastructure it is installed on, keeps what it
observes with its date and its origin, measures its health, and helps the
person who runs it understand, explain and fix it. This manual describes
every screen and how to set up a new installation.

## The basics

### Two addresses

AIStack answers on two addresses:

- **the public address** (for example `https://aistack.<domain_name>`),
  reachable from the internet: the console, help, legal notice, licence,
  Settings and — once signed in — Architecture and the Health Cockpit;
- **the local-network address** (for example `http://GIGABYTE:8186`):
  everything above, plus the screens that show the infrastructure's history
  or act on it (CPU priority, music selection, network discovery, the
  troubleshooting assistant, the Time Machine).

On the console, a card's border says where its screen is reachable from:
blue-grey for the local network, gold for the internet.

### Signing in

The **Sign in** button, at the top of every page, opens the identity
provider's sign-in page (Pocket ID). Once signed in, you come back to the
page you asked for, with your name at the top. **Sign out** ends the session
on AIStack and at the provider.

On the local network only, a **fallback account** lets you in when the
provider or the internet is down: the **Fallback administrator** link next
to *Sign in*. After five wrong passwords within fifteen minutes, it refuses
every attempt until those fifteen minutes have passed.

A session ends after **8 hours without activity** or **7 days** at most.

### Profiles

| Profile | Who | May |
|---|---|---|
| **Administrator** | a member of the provider's `aistack_admins` group, or the fallback account | read everything and act |
| **User** | any other provider account | read everything, change nothing |
| *Signed out* | — | the console, help, legal notice, licence, language |

A refused action shows a page saying why. The profile is read at each
sign-in: removing someone from the group at the provider takes effect at
their next sign-in.

### Language

The flags at the top of every page change the language of every page;
Settings lets you choose it explicitly. The choice is kept in this browser by
a single cookie.

### Tooltips

Every button, link and field says what it does when hovered.

## The console

The home page. It gathers:

- **the health frame**: the score out of 100 from the Health Cockpit's last
  run, the technical-debt mark (the lower it is, the more domains carry debt)
  and one badge per domain — green without findings, red with at least one
  (a click opens its detail in the Cockpit), grey when the domain is not
  measured on this host;
- **the screens' cards**, grouped by address (local network or internet),
  each with a description;
- **the left column**: help (which leads to this manual), the legal notice,
  the licence and the sources (code repositories and Docker image).

The console is regenerated every time the web service starts, and by
`python -m aistack.cli.console_render`.

## Settings

Opened from the **Settings** button at the top of every page.

- **Interface language** — for everyone.
- **My profile** — once signed in: name, e-mail, profile, groups, when and
  how the session was opened, when it ends at the latest.
- **Open sessions** — administrators: who is signed in, how, on which
  address, since when, last activity, and **Close** to end someone else's
  session.
- **Sign-in journal** — administrators: sign-ins, refusals, sign-outs and
  fallback failures of the last 30 days.
- **Disks and mounts** — administrators: the disks and mount points the
  server sees, their size, their free space, and what AIStack already keeps
  on them.
- **Where AIStack's data lives** — administrators: where all of
  AIStack's data (histories, explications, graph, sessions, generated
  pages) lives, as one block. Choose another disk from the list: it
  holds only the local disks open for writing, with their free space,
  except the one the data is already on (no network share: the
  sessions database is not safe there). The data goes to `AIStack/data`:
  a dedicated `AIStack` directory at the root of the disk. AIStack refuses a disk without enough room,
  then records the choice and shows **the commands to run**, in order. It never moves
  anything itself. On a git installation the services are stopped, the
  data copied, `reports/generated` becomes a link to the new directory
  and the old one is kept as `reports/generated.avant-deplacement`;
  with Docker, the data is copied and `AISTACK_DATA_DIR` is declared in
  `.env`. The state turns to **done** once the services are restarted.

## Architecture

*Sign-in required.* The map of the infrastructure, regenerated by
`python -m aistack.cli.architecture_render`:

- the topology graph of the containers and their real dependencies
  (`depends_on:`);
- the external network topology and each machine's hardware;
- each machine's live state as reported by Beszel;
- the **real-time CMDB**: every declared HTTP endpoint, asked at generation
  time.

## Health Cockpit

*Sign-in required.* Regenerated by `python -m aistack.cli.health_render`.

The score out of 100 combines seven domains, each weighted per `OPS-0008`:
storage, services, backups and disaster recovery, GPU, restore tests,
persistent state (does every service that keeps data have a real backup
strategy?) and inventory gaps (declared services against containers really
discovered). A separate card gives the technical debt: each domain
with at least one debt finding takes 15 points off, once (`OPS-0008`),
and its findings are listed.
A service started only when needed is declared with `on_demand: true` in
`service_categorization.yml`: stopped, it is not counted as an inventory
gap.

AIStack's own code that is no longer used first goes through a
**quarantine** (`OPS-0012`) before it is deleted: it stays in place for six
weeks, and any use of it is recorded. While it is not empty, the quarantine
counts in the technical debt (15 points, once); a line of the card says how
many items it holds, the review date and the number of uses recorded, and
names those that were used or are ready to be deleted. The detail:
`python -m aistack.cli.quarantine_report` (with Docker:
`docker compose exec web python -m aistack.cli.quarantine_report`).

Each finding gives its subject, its interpretation, the proposed fix and its
confidence. The **Diagnose with the AI assistant** link opens that finding in the troubleshooting
assistant, on the local network.

After a change that moves the score, regenerate the console and the Cockpit
together, so that they show the same figure:

```
python -m aistack.cli.console_render && python -m aistack.cli.health_render
```

## CPU priority

*Local network, sign-in required, saving reserved to administrators.* Each
container gets a class:

- **Ignored, left untouched** — no limit;
- **Throttled** — limited to little CPU (the default is shown at the top; a
  field changes it for this container);
- **Priority** — gets more CPU while active, with its activity detector
  (Jellyfin playback in progress, or sustained CPU use) and its normal and
  boosted limits.

**Save the classification** writes `resource_priority.yml`. The
`aistack-resource-priority-monitor` service then applies the limits: when a
priority application becomes active, throttled containers are limited; once
it is quiet again, after the grace window, everything goes back to normal.
Every decision is recorded and visible in the Time Machine.

## Synchronise the smartphone

*Local network, sign-in required, syncing reserved to administrators.*
Choosing, in the music library, what is copied to the phone:

- tick folders in the tree (search and *Collapse all* / *Expand all* help);
  a sub-folder of a ticked folder is included;
- the **Capacity** card compares the selection with the declared capacity
  and refuses a selection that is too large;
- **Synchronise with the smartphone** applies the selection in the target folder;
  Syncthing then sends it to the phone, whose progress shows when the device
  is declared.

## Network discovery

*Local network, sign-in required, changes reserved to administrators.* The
list of SSH user names the network scan tries, in order, on every machine it
finds, to discover the Docker containers there. Add only real accounts you
control: the scan tries them unattended.

The scan itself runs from the server:
`python -m aistack.cli.network_docker_discover`.

## Troubleshooting assistant

*Local network, sign-in required, actions reserved to administrators.* The
list of real faults detected right now by the Health Cockpit and the CPU
watch. For one of them:

1. **Start →**: a local AI model (Ollama) reads the finding and proposes an
   explanation and a next step — never a source of truth;
2. the assistant guides you step by step; **Need help opening a terminal?**
   explains how to connect to the server;
3. for the one fix AIStack knows how to apply in one click (declaring a
   container "throttled" in the CPU priorities), **Apply** writes it, then a
   new diagnosis checks it really worked.

Every AI reasoning is kept in the subject's history.

## Time Machine

*Local network, sign-in required.* What AIStack observed, kept in a
provenance graph (PROV-O). The graph is rebuilt in full, on demand, from the
histories:

```
python -m aistack.cli.timemachine_rebuild
```

Until it has been built, every page says so and gives that command. Four
views, linked by the menu at the top of every page:

### Streams

One stream per history (CPU decisions, Docker observations, Docker events,
file drift, image digests, package inventories, AI reasoning…). A stream
opens the list of its instants; an instant opens everything the graph knows
about it.

### Tree

Network → Host → Stack → Container, read live from Docker and Compose, with
a search by name. **History** opens an element's node in the graph; **View
in ribbon** opens its chronology.

### Ribbon

Every recorded instant on a time axis, one lane per stream, filterable by
stream and by subject. A click or a drag opens the nearest instant; a group
of instants too close together unfolds into a list. The full list below is
paginated and grouped by stream.

### Explications

Every subject that has an explanation — the *why* — with the status,
confidence, author, validator, date and number of versions of its current
version. The **Status** filter shows the explanations to handle
(*Proposed*), validated or discarded; the search filters by name. A subject
opens its Why page.

### A node

A node's page shows three columns: the network tree, the subject's
chronology (with **Reconstitute at this instant** and **See the why**), and
the node's known facts with its provenance diagram.

### Reconstitute

What was known about a subject at a given instant, stream by stream: the
latest observation at or before that instant, or "nothing observed yet".
AIStack never restores anything from this screen.

### Why

Every version of a subject's explanation, from the oldest to the current
one, with its confidence, status, author, validator and, for a discarded
version, who discarded it and why. Explanations come from four imported
sources (commit messages, restore-test comments, the AI's answers, session
notes) and from administrators.

An administrator has three actions; each adds a version, none deletes:

- **Write** — a new text, starting from the current version; recording the
  same text twice is refused;
- **Validate** — confirm the current version, as a second author;
- **Discard** — set the current version aside, with a required reason.

The rules depend on the instance's **phase**:

| | Development phase | Production phase |
|---|---|---|
| A written text | is validated as recorded, in your name | waits for another person's validation |
| Validating one's own text | allowed | refused |
| Deleting a subject's versions | `python -m aistack.cli.explications_admin purge <subject>` | impossible |

If someone changed the explanation since your page was opened, your action
is refused and the page says so: read the current version, then try again.
The graph sees each action at the next `timemachine_rebuild`.

## Setting up a new installation

The prerequisites and precautions in detail are in the *How to install*
section of the repository's README. In order:

1. **Prerequisites**: a Linux host with systemd, Docker and Docker Compose,
   Python 3.13, git, an OpenID Connect provider (Pocket ID) reachable over
   HTTPS, and a reverse proxy with TLS for the public address. Optional:
   Ollama (AI), Syncthing (music), Beszel (metrics).
2. **The code**: clone the repository into `/srv/aistack`, create the Python
   environment (`python3.13 -m venv .venv`, then
   `.venv/bin/python -m pip install -e ".[dev]"`), and check that `pytest -q`
   and `python -m aistack.cli.knowledge_integrity` pass.
3. **The declarations**: under `src/aistack/*/definitions/` (with Docker,
   in `./config`, see below), at least the
   host name and the two ports (`instance_config.yml`), the provider's
   address and the public address (`authentication.yml`), the topology, the
   network to scan and the console's links. Keep `phase: development` while
   setting up.
4. **The identity provider**: a group named exactly `aistack_admins` (its
   *name*, not its display name), a confidential client with PKCE and **four
   addresses** — the two callback URLs (`/auth/callback`) and the two
   after-logout URLs (`/console.html`), for the public and the local-network
   address, written letter for letter.
5. **The secrets** in `.env.web`, without displaying them: the client's ID
   and secret, then the fallback account's password with
   `python -m aistack.cli.web_admin_password >> .env.web`; then
   `chmod 600 .env.web`.
6. **The services**: adapt the user and path in the units under
   `deploy/systemd/`, copy them to `/etc/systemd/system/`, then
   `systemctl enable --now` `aistack-web` and the collectors.
7. **The reverse proxy**: publish **only** the public port (8183); never the
   local-network port.
8. **First pages**: `architecture_render`, `health_render`, `console_render`,
   the explanation imports, then `timemachine_rebuild`.
9. **Check**: open the local-network address and sign in; in Settings, your
   profile must read **Administrator**.
10. **Move to production** once other people use AIStack:
    `phase: production` in `instance_config.yml`, then restart
    `aistack-web`.

### With Docker

The Docker image runs AIStack itself: the repository's
`docker-compose.yml` starts the web application and the five
collectors, six services of one image. The prerequisites, the identity
provider and the reverse proxy are the same; Python, git and the
systemd units are no longer needed.

```
mkdir -p /srv/aistack && cd /srv/aistack
# copy docker-compose.yml and .env.example here from the repository
cp .env.example .env        # AISTACK_VERSION, your user and group ids, the Docker socket's group
mkdir -p config data
touch .env.web && chmod 600 .env.web
docker compose pull && docker compose up -d
```

At first start, `./config` receives every declaration AIStack ships,
with the reference host's values. AIStack starts anyway, and **every
page shows "⚠ To configure"** at the top: that link opens the **First
start** page (`/setup`), which says what is still to declare, in which
file, and the values in use today:

1. **the host and its ports** — `./config/instance_config.yml`;
2. **the identity provider and the public address** —
   `./config/authentication.yml`;
3. **the OpenID Connect client** — its ID and secret in `.env.web`;
4. *recommended*: **the fallback account** —
   `docker compose exec web python -m aistack.cli.web_admin_password`,
   then paste the line into `.env.web`.

After each change, `docker compose restart`. A declaration counts as
done once its file has been edited; a file you put in `./config`
yourself before the first start is never reported. Once everything
required is declared, the link disappears.

Every host directory your declarations name (backup disks, music) is
added at the end of `x-aistack`'s `volumes:`, at the same path,
read-only.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Invalid callback URL" at the provider**: the callback of the address
  used is not declared identically on the client.
- **"not allowed" at the provider**: your account is in no group allowed on
  the client.
- **User profile instead of Administrator**: the provider's group is not
  named exactly `aistack_admins`.
- **Signing in no longer answers**: use the fallback account on the local
  network, then read `journalctl -u aistack-web -n 50 --no-pager`.
- **The Cockpit and the console show different scores**: regenerate them
  together.
- **A Time Machine page says the graph does not exist**: run
  `python -m aistack.cli.timemachine_rebuild`.
